## How to read it

- Sign convention: negative = better (lower RMSE, seconds). "Realised" is the leaderboard score minus the best score before that upload (not minus the previous upload when the previous one lost).
- Upload time: the `processed_at` stamp on the leaderboard (UTC). Scores are the leaderboard scores for team jolly-lobster.
- "Predicted" counts only what our upload log shows was written before that version's score. Where nothing was written, the cell says "none recorded" and we have not reconstructed one. Where several estimates were recorded they are all listed and labelled. Before v138 "pre-registered" means written in the running log before the upload. The log file has been in version control since 2026-09-25 01:35 UTC (the evening of 2026-09-24 local time), but before v138 its entries were committed in batches after the uploads they describe (those of 2026-10-01 22:05 to 2026-10-03, which cover v129 to v137, in one commit on 2026-10-03 20:40 UTC), so the commit history does not show when such an entry was written.
- Judges named in the table: **fold** = leave-one-month-out (LOMO) gain on all 2025 hold-out months, in total-RMSE seconds; **months 1+7** = the same on held-out January and July 2025 only (the months the leaderboard scores); **lens** = our conversion of a fold gain into an expected leaderboard change using the 2026 row footprint; **day fold** and **season judge** = two alternative hold-out schemes used on v121. Rome notes quote hold-out gains with the opposite sign convention (positive = better); those are written out as "gain" below.
- Ratio = realised / predicted. Where a range was recorded the ratio uses the middle of the range. It is shown only where a single comparable figure exists.
- Source of every entry: `docs/LAB-NOTES.md` (chronological log). Entry order in the log was used to decide what came before each score. Caveat on timing: for v119, v120, v135, v136 and v137 the time stamp written on the pre-upload entry is later than the leaderboard processing time, although the entry sits before the scored entry in the log. These entries were committed after the uploads (see "Predicted" above), so we could not confirm the writing time independently. They are counted on entry order and marked with an asterisk (*). For v136 the record shows more: the log entry is stamped 01:00 UTC on 2026-10-03, sixteen minutes after the score was recorded (00:44 UTC), so by its own stamp it was written after the score, although it stands above the score entry. ## Uploads made by script

Most uploads from about v95 to v157 were made by `archive/upload_when_slot.sh`, which waits for a given UTC day, re-checks guards against the best file (row count, ids, correlation above 0.99, mean shift under 5 s, no negative values, fewer than 5 uploads that day, not already uploaded) and uploads; it is in this release. `archive/auto_v94.sh` (in this release) decides a model set by a rule fixed before the last fold of a screen, builds, uploads and polls the score. Three kinds of script are not in this release and are described here instead:

- **An upload queue** (v109 and v111 to v115, 2026-09-25 and 26; the written rules covered upload days 25 to 27 September). It took prepared files in priority order, rejected any whose recorded predicted gain was worse than -0.10 s, took the base as the best-scoring file, and stopped for the day after two consecutive uploads that did not improve the best score. v110 was queued but not uploaded (no slot was left that day). v115, the one upload that did not improve, was the first non-improving upload of the day, so the stop rule was not reached.
- **An adaptive controller** written on 2026-09-24 to keep a component only if its upload beat every earlier score and to add one new component each time. It was stopped before it uploaded anything (its log is empty).
- **Chain wrappers** for v134, v139, v140 and v141 (and, for the base file, v101 and v102) that waited for the previous score and built on the better of two files (if the score is below the best, the new file is the base, otherwise the old one). For v140 and v141 the base was chosen among v137, v138 and v139 by score; v138 and v139 had lost, so the base was v137. These wrappers make the build depend on a score, in the way "built on the best-scoring file" implies.
